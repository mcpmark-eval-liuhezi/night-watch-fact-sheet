## 3) 18th-century story (cross-checked vs. Wikipedia and the Rijksmuseum site)

- The painting was completed in 1642 at the peak of the Dutch Golden Age, commissioned around 1639 by Frans Banninck Cocq and seventeen members of his civic militia guard (schutterij / kloveniers) for the banquet hall of the Kloveniersdoelen in Amsterdam.
- In 1715 (73 years after completion) the painting was moved to Amsterdam's Town Hall, where it was trimmed on all four sides, presumably to fit between two columns; this is common practice before the 19th century. The trim lost two figures on the left side, the top of the arch, the balustrade, and the edge of the step; those portions have not been found.
- A 17th-century copy by Gerrit Lundens (1622–1683), on loan from the National Gallery, London, shows the original composition; in 2021 the painting was exhibited with the trimmed-off sections recreated by AI based on Lundens's copy.
- Notable extras from the web sources not in the record: the dark varnish gave the incorrect impression of a night scene (hence "Night Watch"), removed only in the 1940s; it was attacked with a knife in 1911 (no real damage due to thick varnish), slashed with a bread knife in 1975 (slashes up to 30 cm, restored after four years), and sprayed with acid in 1990 (only damaged the varnish). In July 2019–2020 "Operation Night Watch" restoration began in a public glass enclosure, and in May 2020 the Rijksmuseum published a 44.8-gigapixel image.
