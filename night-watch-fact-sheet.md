## 3) 18th-Century Story (cross-checked vs. Wikipedia + Rijksmuseum)

- Painted: 1642 (completed at the peak of the Dutch Golden Age), commissioned ~1639 by Frans Banninck Cocq and seventeen members of the civic militia (schutterij); 34 figures in total; Rembrandt paid 1,600 guilders.
- Move to Amsterdam Town Hall: in 1715, ~73 years after completion (1715 - 1642 = 73), the painting was moved to the Amsterdam Town Hall and trimmed on all four sides to fit between two columns — a common practice before the 19th century.
- Loss: two figures on the left, the top of the arch, the balustrade, and the edge of the step were lost; the missing portions have not been found. The 17th-century copy by Gerrit Lundens (on loan from the National Gallery, London) shows the original composition.
- Note: the dark varnish gave the false impression of a night scene, hence the name "Night Watch" (the scene is actually daytime). The varnish was removed only in the 1940s.
- Sources: English Wikipedia ("The Night Watch") and the Rijksmuseum collection page (https://www.rijksmuseum.nl/en/collection/SK-C-5). Britannica was bot-blocked; the museum's own page plus Wikipedia suffices as two reputable sources.
